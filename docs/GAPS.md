# Nullmere Online against Rise of Kingdoms: the gap list

The bar, set by ael on October 30: Nullmere Online is to be a complete game
on the level of Rise of Kingdoms or better, native to the Mac. Everything
below is scored against Rise of Kingdoms, honestly: anything 2D, a
placeholder, stubbed or simulated scores under ten. The rules hold
throughout: one shared realm and nothing played alone, the launcher titled
exactly Nullmere Online, and every asset original. This list is kept
current as the work goes; each line says what is there now, not what is
planned, and what CI has shown of it. The rules of every system are in
[PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md).

## What closes each gap

Each item is done when it is real, in the shipped app, tested, and shown in
CI (a test, a figure, or a screenshot). Ticked items are done.

### 1. Live realm, real multiplayer
- [x] Several keepers' clients, as shipped, play together in CI against a real SpacetimeDB server: they arrive, see each other, trade, ally, speak, march on each other and fight
- [x] A keeper's client on a real SpacetimeDB server in CI sees its hold in the world's 3D models as the realm keeps it: a sawpit ordered raised rises in its scaffold, the sawyers' rig at work beside it, every piece drawn within its budget (and the same played through the fake server on Linux)
- [x] A live connection: the realm's updates pushed to the client over SpacetimeDB's WebSocket subscription, the rows themselves (the status, the keeper's view and its parts, the answers to their requests) taken as the server pushes them, so a keeper asks the server nothing after a publication; reading the realm kept only for a line that can't be kept or goes quiet
- [x] Words between keepers: a realm channel and an alliance channel
- [x] A full realm of keepers at once: every seat of a ninety-six-seat realm taken together by its own client, as shipped, on a real SpacetimeDB server in CI (run 634): all seated and shown their own House's view of the one world in 73.8 s while the realm takes in a quarter of its seats each watch of 20 s, every client seeing the watch turn 7.9 s after the last made ready, and one keeper's words reaching every other in 6.3 s (74 publications in 99 calls, 41.9 MB), in a debug build with coverage counted. Built as shipped, on a local SpacetimeDB server, every client saw the watch turn 1.1 to 1.7 s after the last made ready and heard the words in 0.7 to 0.8 s over two runs, taking the rows the server pushed. Publications too large for one request go in calls of about a megabyte, the world and every answer in the first; the pages every keeper is told alike are kept once for all their views; a keeper's link never cancels a request in flight
- [x] One workflow that publishes the realm to maincloud once the maintainer adds the token (the sign-in, and the machine the steward runs on, are the maintainer's)
- [x] Marches and battles that play out across the watch, not resolved at its turn: twelve paces a watch, a host striking as it arrives, stewards acting through it
- [x] Battles that last: in the realm, companies in battle exchange blows again at the paces after the first, until one side falls or draws off
- [x] Armies counted in troops: every company so many at its first muster by kind and tier (Spears 1,200 to 2,400), and those drilled into it since, as many standing as its health allows; battles told in the troops each side slew and lost; a House's army totalled
- [x] Battles of whole armies: fields of battle in which every company near strikes and is struck pace by pace, whole armies drawn in, allies fighting on the side they strike for, each side's companies and troops reported as one battle, shown on the map with each side's troops standing; both sides' war banners at each field's heart, leaning as each gives ground and laid flat when it breaks
- [x] Armies by the hundred thousand: companies of war drilled at the Muster Halls take on troops watch by watch for grain, up to what the House's best hall and the company's commander can lead (Spears V with a hall of V and a commander of the thirtieth level lead 218,400); in 96 ages of twelve seats a realm's armies came to 3,190,000 troops at the median as the age ended
- [x] The Still Hour (invention): twice a moon, at its fourth and nineteenth nights, the black lake lies still as glass and every company of war standing on its shore is mirrored for every House to see, where it stands, whose and how many; a House that would not be seen casts a ripple-stone from a hold on the shore beforehand, for 8 moonsalt, one a Still Hour, and none of its companies is mirrored; the mirror fades with the watch after. Stewards at war with companies on the shore cast stones the watch before (the wary and cunning always); the keeper is told it, hears it, sees the mirrored companies faint on the map where the lake showed them, casts a stone from a shore hold's card, and finds it in the Guide
- [x] Commander pairings (RoK's primary and secondary): a second commander rides with a company that has its leader, adding half its edges and a point more to attack and defence when the two are of one archetype; it learns at half the pace, is carried home wounded with its leader, takes the lead if the leader is called back; stewards send a commander with no company to lead to ride second, its own kind first; the Commanders panel's Ride second
- [ ] Battles of hundreds of thousands. In 96 ages of twelve seats, a realm's greatest battle comes to 144,300 troops at the median and 387,600 at the most, over 100,000 in 81 of the ages and over 200,000 in 13; in 8 ages of forty-eight seats, to 166,000 and 248,300. The rules hold such battles (a test meets two armies of eight companies of 25,000 as one field of 400,000), but the stewards' armies never meet whole: a battle takes in about a fifth of its two sides' armies, the rest standing in their holds or marching elsewhere. Wider fields of battle, and a dozen ways for stewards to gather before a siege or come to their allies' battles, were tried and measured over whole ages; none was kept, each leaving the battles no larger or tilting which tempers win. Tried again (November 2): siege rallies called by any House of an alliance at war, allies sending twice the companies, and a House drawing up to six free companies to its own threatened gates; over 32 ages against 32 without it, the greatest battle fell (median 122,725 troops against 135,142; over 100,000 in 25 ages against 30) and the steadfast won 11 to 6, so it stays behind its switch, off. The rule it needed stays: a House may keep its own gates as well as an ally's. Tried next: alliance fronts, every member at war with the House its leader marches on going for the leader's own target; over 64 ages against 64 without, the greatest battles grew at their peak (282,598 troops; five ages over 200,000 against three) but fell in the middle (median 131,042 against 141,500), the tempers' wins unmoved; kept behind its switch, off. Measured anew (November 5) with BattleSizeTests (NULLMERE_BATTLE_AGES=16, twelve seats, release build): median 113,414 troops, most 289,335, over 100,000 in 11 of 16 ages and over 200,000 in 2. The greatest battle follows the age's length: ages ended by watch 120 peak at 65,000 to 83,000, those lasting to watch 200 at 218,000 to 289,000, so armies are still growing by drill when short ages end. RoK's battles reach hundreds of thousands
- [x] Realms of many Houses: a realm raised with up to ninety-six seats, its lake as much wider as keeps each seat its country (a radius of 72 hexes for forty-eight seats, 102 for ninety-six, against 36 for twelve), its seats a walk from the shore that lengthens with the lake so they keep their room (eight hexes or more apart at forty-eight, seven at ninety-six, on every lake's shape); the twelve elder Houses and eighty-four younger ones, each with an original name and a colour of its own that reads as words, each House's land washed in its colour on the map; a lake of twelve seats or fewer made exactly as before
- [ ] Kingdoms of thousands. A realm's watch grows with its seats and its age, still a little faster than its seats. Every seat a keeper's and kept by its caretaker, in an optimised build on a four-core Linux machine, its rules and stewards play the first ninety watches in 0.5 s for twelve seats, 2.0 s for forty-eight and 5.7 s for ninety-six, against 0.8, 5.6 and 16.0 s on the same machine before the stewards stopped seeking the lake's deepest water through every tile at every look in the full moon's walks on the mirror, every keeper's riddle through the whole Chronicle, and every hold for the land's claims (November 6); the ninetieth watch for ninety-six seats takes 78 ms against 102 (1.0 s when first measured, 143 ms before the stewards stopped copying holds and hashing in their looks at war, 120 ms before the realm kept its companies on a board of where each stands and its holds by hex and by House). Past the cap, as a probe: 384 seats' watches near the fiftieth take about 350 ms against 600, still more than twice as much for twice the seats, and 768 seats' 0.99 s in 114 MB (1.31 s in 275 MB before each House kept the ground it has explored as a bit to a tile); a company in a realm of ninety-six seats costs the watch under twice what one in a realm of twelve does (0.034 ms against 0.019), where it cost five times as much. Who stands on a hex, the hold there and a House's holds and companies are found at once, the realm keeping them beside its lists; more than half of a ninety-six seats' watch is now its stewards' campaigns, each company weighing where it marches, and that still grows a little faster than its seats. Every keeper's view is worked out side by side, one to a core, and costs what is theirs alone more than the realm's size: what every view takes alike (every House's strength and might, the map as one who has explored none of it knows it, the view of a keeper of no House in its parts) is worked out once a publication, each view's map fogged from the tiles its House has explored rather than tile by tile, and the map sent 2,048 tiles to a page; every page just as a keeper of no House is told it is taken as written there, so a keeper's view at 768 seats writes 10 of its 9,579 parts, 178 KB, for itself. A view and its parts take 4.9 ms at ninety-six seats against 11.2, 7.8 ms at 384 against 43, and 24 ms at 768 against 102 (as a probe, on one core). Still open for kingdoms of thousands: the realm keeps a relation for every two of its Houses (295,000 at 768 seats, 5 MB of every view's 7.6), and keeping only those two Houses have had dealings in won't be enough while the Sundering sets every House at war with every one across the water and back (a quarter of every pair after its first season): it wants the shores' war kept as the kingdoms', not pair by pair. The pages every keeper is told alike are kept once for all their views, not in each, a keeper's own House and the works under way in their holds apart from the pages of every House and every hold, and the deeds done in pages of their own: at ninety-six seats every view shown whole comes to 5.8 MB written against 40.6, and a watch's turn to 2.8 MB against 13.5 (a probe's measure at the fiftieth watch), 2.0 to 2.1 MB at the third against 3.1 to 4.3. What is left is each keeper's own: of a turn at the fiftieth watch, the map they explored 1.3 MB (in pages of 2,048 tiles), their House and holds 0.6 MB, their head 0.4 MB, the companies they see 0.4 MB. A publication goes to the server in calls of about a megabyte (it takes 2 MiB at most), the world's changed parts and every answer in the first so they are kept together: at ninety-six seats a watch's turn rewrites 0.96 MB of a world of 1.26 MB at its fiftieth watch, and the world comes to 1.69 MB by its age's end (watch 136), most of it every House's pages and the head (190 KB and 160 KB), so a realm much wider wants the world itself sent in calls and kept aside until the last. Each cut that got it here kept the same worlds byte for byte; the commits tell them. RoK's kingdoms hold thousands of governors
- [ ] The realm up for everyone on maincloud. Everything is ready but what only the maintainer can give:
  1. `spacetime login` on the maintainer's machine, then the token from `spacetime login show --token` saved as the repository secret `SPACETIME_TOKEN`;
  2. Actions → Publish the realm → Run workflow (database `nullmere`), which publishes the module to https://maincloud.spacetimedb.com/nullmere;
  3. the steward started as the same identity on a machine that stays up: `scripts/raise-realm.sh maincloud` (or the `Dockerfile.steward` image), as docs/GO-LIVE.md step 1 says;
  4. then Actions → Live realm smoke (also run each day) joins two keepers' clients to the live realm and checks each sees the other's House and hears the other speak. Until the realm answers it warns and passes.
  The same two-client smoke runs on every push against a standalone realm raised by the same one command with its steward in the background. From this agent's container maincloud can't be reached at all (the proxy refuses the connection with 403), so the live realm hasn't been seen from here.

## Beyond Rise of Kingdoms: what nobody has shipped

Direction of October 31: cover Rise of Kingdoms whole, then go past it with
mechanics of Nullmere's own, from what no other game of its kind has: a still
lake under a moon, an on-device model on every keeper's Mac, and a realm that
persists, age after age. Each is ticked when it is real, tested and shown in
CI; [PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md#what-only-nullmere-has) has the rules of each.

- [x] **Ferries of the Still.** Companies poled across the lake between a
  House's own shore holds in one watch, for timber: barges seen on the water
  by whoever sees either shore, turned back if the landing falls meanwhile.
  Rules, realm, a contextual line in the selection panel and the barge drawn
  in 3D with its ferryman and lantern.
- [x] **The Moonford.** The moon keeps a calendar of thirty watches, and about
  each full moon from the fifty-fifth watch the tide lays bare a ford of
  shingle across the lake for ten watches, where the two kingdoms of a
  Sundering meet; the water takes back whoever still stands on it as the
  moon wanes. Over 48 ages it leaves how ages end where it was and makes
  battles a little larger (median 85,020 troops against 79,206).
- [x] **Edicts of the realm.** Laws a realm votes for itself (the Moon's
  Peace, the Harvest Truce, Safe Conduct, Holy Ground), every House voting
  by its might, and kept by the server for twenty watches: politics held by
  the rules, not by players' good faith.
- [x] **Ballads.** Verse of a House's own deeds, written on its keeper's Mac
  by its on-device model and sung to the realm's songbook: verse no server
  wrote and no other keeper's Mac made.
- [x] **Bottles on the lake.** Words set adrift from the shore, carried by
  the lake's current to whoever finds them: anonymous or signed, offers,
  warnings and lies, a way to speak to strangers that no chat has.
- [x] **Drowned ages.** The holds of the age before lie drowned in the new
  lake as named ruins, searched for what their works held: the realm's
  history is its map.
- [x] **The broken Spire.** The Moonspire an age raised highest stands broken
  in the next, a holy site whose holders build for less and sooner, and
  whose keeper names the realm's titles.
- [x] **Dreams of the Still.** At the dark of each moon, each keeper's House
  is shown one true thing it cannot see, chosen by the realm and told in the
  Still's voice by the keeper's own Mac: intelligence no spy brings, from a
  model no server runs.
- [x] **The Augury.** A market in foresight kept by the realm: keepers
  foretell the realm's course and stake moonsalt on it, others back or stand
  against them, and the realm judges by what comes to pass. No odds are set
  and no player judges.
- [x] **Whispers on the water.** Unsigned rumours whose truth the realm alone
  knows until a keeper's Lanterns find it out; a false one names its teller
  to the whole realm. Deception and reputation with no game master.
- [x] **The Remembering.** At each half moon, a riddle of the realm's own
  past, set in the Still's voice by the keeper's Mac: the realm's history
  made into play, age after age.
- [x] **The Eclipse.** At the eaten moon the drowned age walks again, its
  greatest holds rising as companies that bear their keepsakes: what keepers
  built in one age comes back to be fought for in the next.
- [x] **Orders in your own words.** A keeper writes what they would have
  done as they would say it, and their Mac's model, as the House's clerk,
  reads it into the order book, each line checked against the keeper's own
  view and given only when the keeper gives it. The model only reads; the
  realm judges.
- [x] **Letters to the age to come.** Sealed under a hold's stones, found in
  its drowned ruin in the next age and read to every House: the realm's past
  speaking to its present in its own keepers' words.
- [x] **Standing orders in your own words.** What a House should do while
  its keeper is away, told in plain words and followed by the realm's
  caretaker: the House played as its keeper would have it while their Mac is
  shut. In Rise of Kingdoms an absent player's troops stand idle.
- [x] **Prisoners who talk.** A commander felled by a keeper's company is
  taken prisoner, questioned for true things of their own House, worked out
  by the realm and spoken in the prisoner's voice by the captor's Mac, and
  ransomed or let go.
- [x] **Oaths the realm keeps.** A keeper swears in their own words to keep
  the peace with a House or send it goods, and the server holds their House
  to it: a promise between players made a binding contract of the world.
- [x] **False reflections.** The lake can be taught to lie: from a hold by
  the water a House casts a false reflection on a stretch of shore for
  moonsalt, three at most to a Still Hour, and at the next Still Hour the
  lake mirrors a company of its own standing there though none is, a
  glimpse no other House can tell from the true; only eyes on that shore
  see it empty, and a company come to stand there is mirrored as it is.
  The House's secret, in view and tidings; drawn by the lake's mirror as
  every glimpse is; under By the water.
- [x] **Listening stones.** The black lake carries sound: a House sinks a
  stone of moonsalt in the water before a hold by the lake (three at most),
  and each watch it hears every company of war of another House that has
  marched within six tiles of it out of the House's sight, only by its
  quarter and whether near or far, never whose; two stones cross their
  bearings on a march. A sounding is a bearing with no position, so fog of
  war becomes something to triangulate. Its House's alone, in view and in
  tidings; a stone is lost with its hold; drawn in 3D, a moonsalt-veined
  standing stone on a cairn in the water, its ripples turning out about it,
  faster once it has heard; a dread note heard when it hears; wary stewards
  at war sink one.
- [x] **The Lyceum** (Rise of Kingdoms' Lyceum of Wisdom): twice in every
  ten watches the realm's scholars sit and every keeper's House is set the
  same five questions on the lake's lore, drawn by the realm from its
  studies, items and omens, the right answer among four by the draw. Each
  House answers each once, its answers its own until the Lyceum rises;
  then the realm judges every House that answered, pays four moonsalt for
  each answer right and 20, 12 and 6 more to the three that answered best,
  and tells the whole realm the standings. A card in the left column asks
  one question at a time while it sits and nothing is chosen, gone once the
  last is answered; a note of lore on each answer, honours or coin as it
  rises. Ruled in the core (LyceumTests), the card tested on the Mac, and
  played between two clients over the fake realm and over a real
  SpacetimeDB 2.13 server (sitTheLyceum): the same questions to both, each
  blind to the other's answers, one judgement told to both and both paid.
  Its hall stands in the world: raised as it first sits on the open land
  nearest the lake's heart, on no House's ground, a domed round house of
  pale stone on ten columns with braziers and lecterns at its doors
  (LyceumHall, in budget); while the scholars sit an orrery of moonsalt
  rings turns over its dome's eye and five lights circle it, dark and still
  between (StageTests); both clients see the one hall lit over the real
  server. Still open: the stewards sitting it.
- [x] **The Calm of the Still.** The black lake is a commons every House
  shares and any one can break: each watch that no company of war stands on
  its shore or its water, or crosses it, the Still lies a degree calmer, to
  ten, and every keeper's hold on the shore draws a tenth more moonsalt for
  each degree. One company of war at the water as the watch turns breaks it
  back to nothing for all at once, and a calm of five or more broken names
  its breakers to the whole realm. No House owns it and none can keep it
  alone: peace on the water pays everyone, and war on it is seen. Drawn on
  the water itself, the moon's road narrowing and lying whole as the calm
  deepens; told in a band only to a keeper with a hold on the shore, from
  three watches deep; a dread note to all when it breaks, a clash to its
  breakers. Ruled in the core and tested there, played between two clients
  over the fake realm and over SpacetimeDB in CI.
- [x] **Walking the Mirror.** The Still is a mirror: a company of war on its
  shore steps into its own reflection for 15 moonsalt, once a moon, and as
  the watch turns the realm brings it out on the far shore, its hex
  reflected through the lake's deepest water, or the nearest open ground
  within three tiles of it. The water stills where it will come out the
  moment it steps in, told to every keeper, so the shore to be come upon
  has the watch to make ready; if that shore fills by the turn, the company
  comes back out where it stepped in. The lake's geometry is the map of
  these walks, and a false reflection on a shore can now be read as one.
  Through the realm's one request table and reducer; a button on a company's
  panel only while it stands on the shore; played between two clients over
  the fake realm and over SpacetimeDB in CI. Drawn in 3D: the walking
  company's image already standing in the stilled water where it will come
  out, in its House's colour, seen from every shore. Cunning and ambitious
  stewards at war walk a company across when the far shore lies within
  three tiles of a foe's hold.

2. The depth of systems, rules first (tested on Linux), then the realm and the interface for each.
3. Animation, on the models as they land.
4. The live connection and the multiplayer test.

6. The onboarding over all of it.
7. Realms meeting across their borders: the Frontier.
8. Armies by the hundred thousand: drill at the Muster Halls.
9. The land's undergrowth, and fine work on the keeps, walls and works, drawn near.
10. Realms of many Houses: up to ninety-six seats round a wider lake.
11. A wide realm drawn only where the view reaches.
12. A wide realm's watch: the stewards' work done over and over cut; the Sundering's shores at war; keepers' views sent in parts and worked out with each march's way found once.
13. The trailer, filmed in the game's own renderers.
